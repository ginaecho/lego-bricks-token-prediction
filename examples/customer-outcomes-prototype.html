The decision loop

  1. Describe a project and explicitly list its functionality.
  2. Inspect forecasts, request one recommendation, and approve or reject it by name.
  3. Record the same autonomous cohort's outcomes, including unsuccessful work.
  4. Have a human review the evidence before learning.
  5. Train a candidate, evaluate protected validation outcomes, then explicitly promote only if permitted.
Synthetic performance is not business proof. Demo seeding creates reviewed example outcomes only; it never trains or promotes a model.

Evaluation & promotion gate

Two learning mechanisms

Outcome regression

Predicts satisfaction, SOW quality, staffing fit, impact, AES, ROI, profit, and net benefit. Per-target sample counts and mean absolute error (MAE) describe fit; compare with baseline MAE. These metrics alone are not future accuracy guarantees.

Contextual bandit

Learns which delivery action to recommend using context, logged selection probabilities, and reviewed reward. Evaluation reports support, effective sample size, estimated reward gain, and a lower bound. This is not proof of a causal business benefit.

Keep future validation cohorts untouched by training or iterative tuning. Promotion requires the backend's eligibility gate and a named human. Synthetic evidence demonstrates mechanics only.
